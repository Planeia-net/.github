# Human Writing Standard

Use these rules for all publishable writing.

## Avoid stereotypical AI patterns

- Do not open with generic scene-setting such as "In today's fast-paced world", "In an ever-changing landscape", or equivalent filler.
- Do not overuse rhetorical questions, "whether you're...", "from X to Y", "not just X, but Y", or three-part slogan structures.
- Do not stack near-synonyms merely to sound comprehensive.
- Do not repeat the same point in the introduction, body, and conclusion with slightly different words.
- Do not use headings for every small idea.
- Do not force every section into the same length or grammar pattern.
- Do not end with generic summaries that add nothing new.
- Avoid empty authority phrases such as "it is important to note", "needless to say", "it is worth mentioning", or "the reality is" unless they carry real meaning.
- Avoid vague claims such as "revolutionary", "game-changing", "seamless", "powerful", "robust", "cutting-edge", "transformative", or "next-level" unless evidence makes the term precise.
- Do not invent quotations, anecdotes, customer examples, or named experts to create texture.

## Make prose feel authored

- Start from the reader's actual problem, decision, tension, or task.
- Prefer specific details over abstraction.
- Vary sentence length naturally. Do not alternate lengths mechanically.
- Let paragraphs have different shapes when the argument requires it.
- Use transitions only when they help the reader follow logic.
- Prefer verbs over abstract noun phrases.
- Remove redundant adjectives and adverbs.
- Use examples only when they clarify something and clearly label hypothetical examples as hypothetical.
- Preserve nuance instead of compressing every issue into a false binary.
- When evidence is mixed or limited, write that directly.

## Editing pass

After drafting:

1. Delete sentences that merely announce what the next sentence will say.
2. Remove repeated claims and duplicate modifiers.
3. Replace vague abstractions with concrete nouns, actions, numbers, or conditions where supported.
4. Break any visibly templated sequence if natural prose would be better.
5. Read for rhythm and awkward repetition.
6. Check that confidence matches the evidence.
7. Remove AI-facing language, internal reasoning, prompt references, and process commentary from publishable copy.
