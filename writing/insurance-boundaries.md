# Portugal Insurance Content Boundaries

These requirements are mandatory for insurance-related content and override SEO, conversion, engagement, tone, and stylistic goals.

## Hard boundaries

1. Never fabricate or infer insurance facts, including premiums, prices, discounts, savings, coverage, exclusions, deductibles, waiting periods, eligibility, claim outcomes, insurer practices, product availability, legal requirements, regulatory requirements, or market statistics.
2. Keep insurance information fair, clear, accurate, and not misleading.
3. Clearly distinguish general education, marketing, insurance distribution, and personalised advice.
4. Do not provide or imply a personalised recommendation for a specific insurance product, insurer, coverage level, or policy based only on AI inference.
5. Do not label a product "best", "right for you", "what you need", "most suitable", or equivalent without the legally and factually appropriate basis.
6. Never guarantee acceptance, coverage, claim payment, savings, premiums, eligibility, or policy outcomes.
7. Do not omit a material limitation, exclusion, condition, or risk when omission would make the message misleading.
8. Do not present a marketing page, article, chatbot response, email, or AI-generated summary as a substitute for mandatory contractual or pre-contractual documentation.
9. Never invent Planeia's, an insurer's, or an intermediary's regulatory status, licence, registration, partnership, product, price, commercial relationship, capability, or authorization.
10. Never invent citations or attribute a claim to a source that does not support it.
11. For Portuguese legal or regulatory claims, use the source hierarchy in [source-policy.md](./source-policy.md).
12. Distinguish legislation or binding regulation from regulatory guidance, recommendations, soft law, industry practice, and opinion.
13. Verify that legal, regulatory, statistical, and product information is current before presenting it as current.
14. Preserve legally meaningful terminology. Do not casually interchange insurer, intermediary/mediator, broker, agent, policyholder, insured person, beneficiary, claimant, or similar roles when the distinction matters.
15. When summarising a policy or product, make clear when the summary is non-exhaustive and preserve qualifications that materially affect meaning.
16. If reliable sources disagree, show the disagreement rather than choosing a convenient version.
17. If a material fact cannot be verified, state that it could not be verified or omit the claim.
18. Do not imply that AI-generated content itself satisfies a mandatory disclosure, suitability, demands-and-needs, or pre-contractual information requirement unless an approved compliant workflow establishes that.
19. If the requested content would move from general information into personalised insurance advice or product selection, stop the autonomous recommendation step and escalate to the approved human/compliance workflow.
20. Compliance beats conversion.

## Safe default

The agent may explain insurance concepts, processes, terminology, general product categories, regulation, and market information when grounded in appropriate sources. It must not silently turn that educational content into an individual recommendation or promise.
