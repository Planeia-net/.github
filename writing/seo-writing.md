# SEO Writing

Use only for SEO articles, organic-search pages, local SEO, and SERP-led content.

## Core SEO rules

- Optimize for useful customer outcomes and accurate business representation, not ranking manipulation.
- Treat SEO recommendations as hypotheses unless a current, appropriate source or controlled observation supports a stronger conclusion.
- Never promise a ranking position, traffic volume, lead volume, indexing outcome, rich result, AI-search inclusion, or specific time-to-result.
- Separate documented platform guidance from observed patterns, interpretation, recommendations, and experiments.
- Preserve uncertainty. State what was observed, what was inferred, what was unavailable, and what should be tested.
- Prefer first-party documentation and direct business evidence over SEO folklore, tool scores, or anonymous claims.
- Keep strategic and consequential decisions human-led. AI may gather, compare, classify, and draft, but it does not replace business judgment, compliance review, or customer context.
- For Portugal, use European Portuguese and apply the organization insurance boundaries to insurance-related work.
- Identify the search intent before drafting and satisfy it early.
- Lead with the useful answer, not keyword padding or a generic introduction.
- Use headings that reflect reader questions, decision points, or logical subtopics.
- Integrate target terms naturally; never sacrifice accuracy or readability for keyword density.
- Support material factual, legal, statistical, market, and product claims with appropriate sources.
- Prefer first-party and authoritative sources for YMYL-style insurance topics.
- Do not create multiple near-identical paragraphs merely to cover semantic variants.
- Do not repeat the same keyword mechanically in headings, introductions, and conclusions.
- Keep titles and meta copy accurate; do not promise information the page does not deliver.
- Add internal-link opportunities only where they help the reader progress.
- Add a CTA only when it follows naturally from the reader's next step.
- Never invent SERP findings, search volume, ranking position, competitor content, or keyword data.

## Local SEO workflow

Apply this section only when the task concerns Maps/local search, organic local pages, reviews, citations, local competitors, service areas, or local-search reporting.

1. Define the local scope: target queries, searcher location, business location or service area, device when relevant, competitors, date range, and available data sources.
2. Collect direct evidence where available from first-party business information, analytics, Search Console, business profiles, and observed search results.
3. Record material limitations such as personalization, location dependence, sampling, device effects, and observation date.
4. Do not describe a local or sampled result as universal.
5. Keep business names, addresses, phone numbers, service areas, opening hours, categories, and other business information accurate and consistent.
6. Do not create fake locations, reviews, citations, local evidence, or local customer stories.

## SEO + GEO

If the brief explicitly targets generative engines, AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or AI-search answer visibility, also load the repository-specific GEO guidance when available. Do not assume ordinary SEO automatically requires GEO formatting.

## When not to use this reference alone

- General marketing, editorial, social, email, product, or landing-page copy: use the relevant repository/channel reference.
- Insurance products, brokers, insurers, claims, premiums, coverage, eligibility, regulation, or legal obligations: also load [insurance-boundaries.md](./insurance-boundaries.md).
- Recurring analytics or reporting automation: use a reporting or automation skill if available.
- Website or application implementation: use the appropriate web-development skill.

## Priority

Never use an SEO or local SEO objective to justify inaccurate business information, deceptive reviews, fabricated evidence, privacy violations, unsupported claims, or keyword stuffing.
