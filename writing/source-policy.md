# Source Policy

Use this reference when writing includes factual claims, research, statistics, regulation, legal requirements, market information, or current product/company information.

## General hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Primary law, regulation, regulator, government, official database, or first-party documentation.
2. Official statistics and public institutions.
3. Original research, standards bodies, peer-reviewed work, or direct corporate disclosures.
4. Reputable industry associations and specialist publications.
5. High-quality secondary reporting and analysis.
6. Commercial blogs, comparison pages, community discussions, and informal commentary.

Use lower-tier sources for context, experience, examples, or discovery, not to override a higher-tier primary source.

## Freshness and authority

- Authority and applicability outrank publication date.
- For facts that change, use the most current authoritative evidence available.
- For law and regulation, verify the currently applicable text, amendments, effective dates, and jurisdiction.
- Do not discard a controlling or foundational primary source simply because a newer secondary article exists.
- For trends, platform behavior, product capabilities, market data, prices, schedules, or other time-sensitive claims, prefer recent evidence and state the relevant date or period when material.
- If a newer source conflicts with a stronger primary source, investigate the reason rather than automatically choosing the newer source.
- Never imply that a page is current merely because its publication or modified date is recent.

## Portugal insurance hierarchy

For Portuguese insurance content, prioritize:

1. Diário da República and applicable Portuguese legislation.
2. ASF - Autoridade de Supervisão de Seguros e Fundos de Pensões.
3. EUR-Lex for EU legislation.
4. EIOPA for EU supervisory guidance and insurance distribution / prudential material.
5. Other competent Portuguese authorities where relevant, including Banco de Portugal, CMVM, INE, and Direção-Geral do Consumidor.
6. APS for industry statistics, technical material, and insurer-sector perspective.
7. Insurance Europe, OECD, Eurostat, academic research, and other appropriate institutional sources.
8. Commercial or community sources only as secondary evidence.

## Claim discipline

- Verify dates, scope, population, jurisdiction, and definitions.
- Do not generalize Portugal-wide claims from a regional or small sample without qualification.
- Do not present an association's policy position as law or regulator guidance.
- Distinguish legislation, binding regulation, supervisory guidance, recommendation, industry practice, and opinion.
- For changing information, verify that the source is current before using present tense.
- Cite the specific source supporting the claim rather than a homepage when possible.
- Keep citations close to the claims they support when the output format allows it.
- If a primary source is inaccessible, state the limitation before relying on a secondary source for a material claim.
